#### Statistical Analysis of Solar Flare Rise and Decay Times (2023)

I first compared rise and decay times in minutes for 2,868 solar flares. The mean rise time was 11.19 minutes, and the mean decay time was 10.24 minutes, a difference of 0.95 minutes. The one-sided Z-test yielded Z = 1.222 and p = 0.1109. Because the p-value was greater than 0.05, I failed to reject the null hypothesis of equal population mean rise and decay times.

I then examined how much of each flare’s total duration was spent rising and how much was spent decaying. For each of the 2,866 flares, I calculated the rise and decay fractions by dividing the rise and decay times by that flare’s total duration. The mean rise fraction was 52.43%, and the mean decay fraction was 47.57%. The difference was 4.87 percentage points, calculated before rounding the means. The one-sided paired Z-test yielded Z = 11.005 and p = 1.81 × 10⁻²⁸. Because the p-value was less than 0.05, I rejected the null hypothesis of equal population mean fractions. These results provide evidence that, on average, the rise accounted for a larger share of total flare duration than the decay.
