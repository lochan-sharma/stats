
#### Statistical Analysis of Solar Flare Rise and Decay Times (2023)
I first compared rise and decay times in minutes for 2,868 solar flares. The mean rise time was 11.19 minutes, and the mean decay time was 10.24 minutes, a difference of 0.95 minutes. The one-sided Z-test yielded \(Z=1.222\) and \(p=0.1109\). Because \(p>0.05\), I failed to reject the null hypothesis of equal population mean rise and decay times.

I then examined what share of a flare’s total duration was spent in each phase. For the 2,866 flares included in this analysis, I calculated the rise and decay fractions by dividing each phase’s duration by the total duration of the same flare. The mean rise fraction was 52.43%, and the mean decay fraction was 47.57%, a difference of 4.87 percentage points based on the unrounded values. The one-sided paired Z-test yielded \(Z=11.005\) and \(p=1.81\times10^{-28}\). Because \(p<0.05\), I rejected the null hypothesis of equal population mean fractions. The normalized results provide evidence that the rise phase accounted for a larger share of total flare duration on average
